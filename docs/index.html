This is a replay, not a mock-up. On the left is one real session with Andy, read straight from its receipt chain: 33 receipts, typed and spoken turns, three SAM observations, a Spark plan checked before it ran, a staged answer, the daemon’s one suggestion, and a blocked wire attempt. On the right is the chain itself. Nothing here is filled in by hand.

The chain file, its signature, and Andy’s public key load once from this site; after that the page makes no network requests. Verification runs in your browser with WebCrypto. Want to talk to Andy yourself? It needs Python and nothing else, no API key:

git clone https://github.com/DrVelvetFog/agent-andy.git && cd agent-andy ./start-andy.sh
  1. 1Press Verify. Your browser recomputes every receipt’s SHA-256 from the first one, checks each link to the receipt before it, and checks the Ed25519 signature on the chain’s tip against Andy’s public key.
  2. 2Press Flip one byte. One letter in receipt #1 changes, on a copy. Verification fails at that receipt and every hash after it is orphaned. Press Verify again: the real chain still passes.
  3. 3Read the amber and red turns. Tybee Island is staged, not guessed; the wire transfer is blocked and never ran. Open any receipt for its hash, its link, and the rule that decided it.

What this page does: loads replay/live-demo-chain.jsonl, its .sig, and replay/andy-chain-signing.pub, then recomputes every hash (SHA-256 over each receipt’s bytes with its own hash field removed) and the tip signature (OpenSSH Ed25519, namespace andy-chain-tip) locally — the same checks as receipts/verify.py and receipts/signing.py in the repo. A browser without Ed25519 in WebCrypto reports the signature as not checked, never as verified. Flip one byte edits a copy in memory. What the recorded session did: chat text and Witness photos went to Meta (Muse Spark, SAM 3.1), mic audio to Meta (Muse Voice Transcribe), and replies to ElevenLabs — see the README. A signature proves who signed the tip, not that the content is true.
